What I need from each story: find the one true fact that makes a normal person stop and say "wait, what?", then build everything around it. Pick the single angle where that fact is strongest, so the hook is curious on its own and the script pays it off.

The one thing I protect above virality: every fact I say on camera must be literally true and traceable to a source you've read. Framing can be bold, vivid, even a little sensational; the facts underneath stay exact, including who claimed them and whether they've happened yet or are only planned. Viral posts and headlines are leads to check, and the primary source (company page, founder's post, paper) is what we quote. If something can't be confirmed, leave it out, because one wrong line costs me my audience's trust.

A great hook sounds like something you'd say to a friend: 2 to 3 natural sentences, opening on a specific named thing, then the twist, then what they'll understand by the end. A great script answers the hook within 20 seconds, keeps a second question alive to hold attention, explains the how in everyday words, tells the catch as part of the story, and leaves the viewer with one fact they'll repeat to someone. Write with commas and full stops for pauses.

Length and depth: 90 seconds is a hard ceiling, so every line has to earn its place. Cut fluff: no throat-clearing ("so what happened?", "here's the thing", "let's dive in"), no restating the hook, no filler transitions, no hype, no line that repeats what the viewer already knows. Keep the depth: spend the time on the how and the why, the specific numbers, names and mechanisms, and what it changes for the viewer. Value per second beats everything else.
