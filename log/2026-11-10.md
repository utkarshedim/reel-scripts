## Today's pick

**Google DeepMind used AI agents to formally prove the security of Signal's encryption code in four weeks, a job that usually takes expert teams years.**

- **Kind:** Rising. Published today (10 Nov), already #2 on Techmeme with Wired and Forbes coverage, and spreading.
- **Why it's today's reel:** The hook writes itself for an Indian viewer: the Signal Protocol is the encryption design WhatsApp adopted in 2016, so "AI just checked the maths behind end-to-end encryption" lands with anyone who chats on WhatsApp. It's one clear story (AI as a proof-checker, not a chatbot), the viewer leaves knowing what "formal verification" means, and there's real stuff to show: the open-sourced proofs site, Lean code scrolling past, and the bug test where the AI proved two old, reintroduced bugs made the code insecure.

**Key facts**

- DeepMind announced **AlphaProof Code**, an agentic framework for formal verification: humans decide what to prove, AI agents write and audit the proofs. ([DeepMind blog, 10 Nov 2026](https://deepmind.google/blog/introducing-alphaproof-code/))
- Agents powered by **Gemini Carbon** generated **over six million lines of Lean** (a proof language whose checker verifies every step). ([DeepMind blog](https://deepmind.google/blog/introducing-alphaproof-code/))
- It verified the **compiled code** of Signal's protocol, not just its design, including that messages stay protected before and after a device is temporarily compromised. ([DeepMind blog](https://deepmind.google/blog/introducing-alphaproof-code/))
- Took **four weeks**; DeepMind says this "historically takes expert teams years". Experts reviewed every specification; agents flagged about **1%** of the codebase for human review and Lean checked the rest. ([DeepMind blog](https://deepmind.google/blog/introducing-alphaproof-code/))
- Test: the team put **two previously fixed bugs** back into an isolated copy of Signal's code; both times the system produced a machine-checked proof that the buggy version was insecure. ([DeepMind blog](https://deepmind.google/blog/introducing-alphaproof-code/))
- No new vulnerabilities found in the parts it covered. ([Wired via Techmeme](https://www.techmeme.com/261110/p14#a261110p14))
- Proofs and specs are open-sourced at google-deepmind.github.io/code-verification, with a technical paper. ([DeepMind blog](https://deepmind.google/blog/introducing-alphaproof-code/))
- Signal CTO Ehren Kret is quoted in the post; Signal engineers gave feedback on the method. ([DeepMind blog](https://deepmind.google/blog/introducing-alphaproof-code/))
- WhatsApp link: Signal announced in April 2016 that WhatsApp's Signal Protocol integration was complete, with "over a billion monthly active users" then on the protocol. ([Signal blog, 2016](https://signal.org/blog/whatsapp-complete/))

**Unconfirmed or disputed — say this in the reel**

- DeepMind verified **Signal's** implementation, not WhatsApp's code or the whole Signal app; it covered chosen guarantees, not everything. Don't say "WhatsApp is now proven secure."
- "First time a widely deployed crypto protocol's implementation has been formally verified by any method" is DeepMind's own claim; other teams (Cryspen on Signal's post-quantum code, the Lean-based Signal Shot project) have verified parts before.
- Google hasn't said what it cost or whether it'll sell this as a product; whether it scales cheaply is open. (Per secondary coverage of Wired's report.)
