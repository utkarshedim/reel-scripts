A new ₹300 crore library in Coimbatore put nearly eighteen hundred books on one floor. A writer who checked them says they were made by AI, under fake author names. And yesterday, the Madras High Court ordered a police case.

The library is Periyar Arivulagam. Seven floors, opened last month. On November 6th, Tamil writer Perumal Murugan went up to the fourth floor, and started reading.

He counted about eighteen hundred books from one publisher, Agal Vilakku. Most of them hardbound. And the contents? One book calls Naladiyar a poet and philosopher. Naladiyar isn't a person. It's a collection of four hundred old verses. That book costs one thousand four hundred and forty five rupees. Murugan says a similar book from other publishers costs about three hundred and fifty.

Another one, a history of Tamil Nadu, names the wrong man as the state's first chief minister.

So who wrote these? That's the strange part. Murugan says the author names are fake. The books list no phone number, just an email address. And a petition in court says one name alone, Monica Ravi, appears on a hundred and sixty two books in six months.

His explanation is simple. He says these were made with AI, cheap AI, and printed to look like real books.

And it's not just one library. In Madurai, a book on democracy carries the name of the famous economist Jean Drèze. He says it's fake. He never wrote it.

Yesterday, the High Court told the anti-corruption police to file an FIR, froze the bank accounts of the publisher and its owner, and ordered action, including suspensions, against the officials involved. The judges said heads must roll. Around two thousand of these books have already been pulled off the shelves.

Now the catch. Nobody has forensically proven these were written by AI yet. That's what the writer, the government, the opposition's own petition, and the court are all saying, but the investigation has just begun. And the two big parties are blaming each other for the purchase.

So here's the one thing to remember. AI can now write a whole book in minutes. And if it can get onto a government library shelf, it can get into your cart. Before you trust a book, check who wrote it, and who published it.
