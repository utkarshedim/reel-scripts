# 2026-11-12

## Today's pick

**United Airlines has banned AI agents from booking its tickets: its new contract says it can cancel a booking an AI made, even if you watched and approved every step.**

- **Kind:** Buzzing. WSJ broke it, then Reuters, CBS, ANI and PYMNTS; it's #5 on the Hacker News front page (448 points, 430 comments) and on Techmeme; travel blogs (One Mile at a Time, View from the Wing) have dissected the contract.
- **Why it's today's reel:** Every big AI company is selling "let the agent do it for you" (book, buy, check in). This is the first big airline to write into its contract that you can't, and to give itself the right to cancel the ticket. It's concrete and personal for this viewer (your ticket, your miles, your password), there's a real fight underneath (safety vs. "no new middleman"), and it's easy to show: the press release, the contract line, an AI agent clicking through a booking site.

**Key facts**

- **11 Nov 2026:** United's press release "United Restricts Access for Third-Party AI Agents": "sharing account credentials with personal, third-party, AI assistants can expose sensitive information, and actions taken by these tools may result in unintended purchases, account changes, or use of MileagePlus miles." ([United newsroom](https://united.mediaroom.com/news-releases?item=125496))
- Same release: these actions "have the potential to violate FAA and DOT regulations - as an example, flight check-in requires human acknowledgment that bags do not contain banned substances like explosives." ([United newsroom](https://united.mediaroom.com/news-releases?item=125496))
- Same release: United will restrict access "directly within our digital platforms and indirectly via travel agencies and aggregators", with updates to "booking, ticketing and distribution policies", and "we encourage others to consider" similar measures. ([United newsroom](https://united.mediaroom.com/news-releases?item=125496))
- Contract of carriage, revised 11 Nov 2026, new prohibited practice (Rule 6.J.6): "The use of AI Agents to make, modify or service a Ticket, and/or to purchase any service or product." ([One Mile at a Time](https://onemileatatime.com/news/united-airlines-bans-ai-agents-protect-customers/) · [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/united-bans-ai-agents-from-buying-its-tickets-seats-and-upgrades/) · [View from the Wing](https://viewfromthewing.com/united-airlines-ceo-scott-kirby-bans-ai-agents-from-booking-tickets-doesnt-want-another-middleman/))
- Definition of AI Agent includes "an AI assistant, AI agent or AI-enabled browser", and applies "whether or not Passenger or another individual supervises or approves its actions." ([One Mile at a Time](https://onemileatatime.com/news/united-airlines-bans-ai-agents-protect-customers/) · [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/united-bans-ai-agents-from-buying-its-tickets-seats-and-upgrades/))
- Rule 5.H: United may cancel "reservations made, modified or serviced by AI Agents." ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/united-bans-ai-agents-from-buying-its-tickets-seats-and-upgrades/) · [View from the Wing](https://viewfromthewing.com/united-airlines-ceo-scott-kirby-bans-ai-agents-from-booking-tickets-doesnt-want-another-middleman/))
- Still allowed: AI can search and show United fares; the person has to finish the purchase themselves. United fares stay on Kayak, Hopper and Google Flights. ([InsideFlyer](https://www.insideflyer.com/posts/united-bars-ai-agents-from-completing-flight-bookings/) · [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/united-airlines-bans-ai-agents-booking-flights/))
- Other side: CEO Scott Kirby, at a Chicago event, per WSJ: "To be honest, I don't want to create another middleman." Analyst Henry Harteveldt (Atmosphere Research) told CBS United wants to keep control of booking and avoid the cost of bot searches. ([View from the Wing](https://viewfromthewing.com/united-airlines-ceo-scott-kirby-bans-ai-agents-from-booking-tickets-doesnt-want-another-middleman/) · [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/united-airlines-bans-ai-agents-booking-flights/))
- Others are going the other way: Amazon has blocked Meta's agent Muse while Walmart partnered with it; Expedia and Booking work with OpenAI. ([Reuters via Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/united-airlines-moves-to-block-ai-agents-from-its-platform-over-security-concerns-5019752)) Southwest shows its flights to ChatGPT users. ([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/united-airlines-bans-ai-agents-booking-flights/))

**Unconfirmed or disputed — say this in the reel**

- The contract wording comes from three outlets quoting it verbatim; united.com's contract page wouldn't load for this run.
- United hasn't said how it will detect AI bookings; a contract clause isn't a technical block, and a booking via Expedia may not show whether AI did it. No cancellations have been reported.
- Tickets issued before 11 Nov are covered by the old terms (Rule 3.K, per PPC Land's reading).
- Kirby's "middleman" quote is via WSJ as reported by View from the Wing; the press release gives only safety and security reasons. Motive is opinion (One Mile at a Time suspects it's about stopping AI from tracking and repricing fares).
- Policy is United's alone; Delta and American haven't followed (per Fast Company headline, page not readable).
