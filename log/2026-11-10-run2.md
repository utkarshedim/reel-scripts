# 2026-11-10 (run 2)

## Today's pick

**Amazon's Ring launched Ring Robotics: a $249.99 drone that flies around inside your home to check on alarms, and a $999.99 security robot for shops. The drone is steered from the cloud, not by a brain on board.**

- **Kind:** Buzzing. Announced today (10 Nov) at a New York launch, #2 on Techmeme, covered by Engadget, ZDNET, Tom's Guide and The Shortcut.
- **Why it's today's reel:** "A camera that flies around your living room while you're out" stops anyone mid-scroll, and the story has a real twist the viewer won't guess: the drone has almost no brain of its own, the cloud flies it over your Wi-Fi, and if the Wi-Fi drops it lands. There's lots to show (launch video of the drone leaving its dock, the 3D home map, the rover's periscope), a human angle (Ring's founder says the idea dates to 2016 and an earlier version never shipped), and a catch people will argue about (a flying camera in your bedroom, video going through Ring's cloud). Not sold in India, but the idea travels.

**Key facts**

- Ring Robotics launches with **Ring Drone Cam** ($249.99, ships to US customers **27 January 2027**) and **Ring Rover Cam** ($999.99, US preorders open, ships **16 December 2026**). ([Amazon, 10 Nov 2026](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/devices/ring-drone-cam-rover-cam-security-robot))
- Drone: 2K camera, 148° field of view, two LEDs for low light; stays docked when idle; flies to preset patrol points when a Ring camera, sensor or alarm triggers, on a schedule (needs a Ring Protect plan) or on demand. ([Amazon](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/devices/ring-drone-cam-rover-cam-security-robot))
- Both run on **AI Pilot**, which builds a floor plan from the camera, identifies rooms, suggests patrol points and avoids obstacles; it combines on-device sensors with cloud processing. ([Amazon](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/devices/ring-drone-cam-rover-cam-security-robot))
- Navigation is mostly in the cloud: founder Jamie Siminoff said "the cloud is telling it where to go"; if it loses connectivity it waits, then lands. ([The Shortcut, 10 Nov](https://www.theshortcut.com/p/amazon-ring-drone-camera-ring-rover-cam-ai-pilot)) Engadget: it lands or returns to the dock when battery is low or Wi-Fi drops. ([Engadget, 10 Nov](https://www.engadget.com/2311630/amazon-finally-launches-the-250-ring-drone-cam-and-also-the-ring-rover-cam/))
- Weighs **165 g**; about **8–10 minutes** of flight (Engadget) / **5–8 minutes** (The Shortcut). Both agree: under ten minutes. ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2311630/amazon-finally-launches-the-250-ring-drone-cam-and-also-the-ring-rover-cam/), [The Shortcut](https://www.theshortcut.com/p/amazon-ring-drone-camera-ring-rover-cam-ai-pilot))
- Privacy: camera off while docked, records only when a flight is triggered; propellers are audible by design; video protected by Ring's TAKE encryption. ([Amazon](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/devices/ring-drone-cam-rover-cam-security-robot))
- History: Siminoff says the drone idea dates to 2016; an earlier drone was announced but "just didn't work well enough"; a version with all the extra sensors and compute would have cost over $5,000. ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2311630/amazon-finally-launches-the-250-ring-drone-cam-and-also-the-ring-rover-cam/)) The earlier Always Home Cam was shown in September 2020. ([MobileSyrup, 24 Sep 2020](https://mobilesyrup.com/2020/09/24/amazon-ring-brand-car-alarm-drone-home/))
- Rover Cam: 4K, a periscope camera that extends up to 4 feet, siren, light tower, two-way audio; built on Amazon's Astro robot. ([Amazon](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/devices/ring-drone-cam-rover-cam-security-robot), [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2311630/amazon-finally-launches-the-250-ring-drone-cam-and-also-the-ring-rover-cam/))
- Drone Cam **has not yet been authorized by the FCC** and can't be sold until it is. ([Amazon footnote](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/devices/ring-drone-cam-rover-cam-security-robot))

**Unconfirmed or disputed — say this in the reel**

- It isn't on sale yet: the drone ships in January 2027 and still needs FCC approval. US only; no India launch announced.
- Flight time differs by outlet (5–8 vs 8–10 min); say "under ten minutes."
- When the earlier drone was first shown is reported as 2020, 2021 or 2022 across outlets; say "years ago" or "a drone Amazon showed back in 2020" (dated MobileSyrup piece).
- Amazon doesn't say exactly which parts run on the device versus in the cloud; the "cloud flies it" line is Siminoff's own description.
- How safe TAKE encryption is, given video goes through Ring's cloud for AI processing, is Amazon's claim; not independently checked.
