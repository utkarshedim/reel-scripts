Jensen Huang, the leather-jacket guy who built Nvidia, just quit, after 33 years. He admits he cheated on his wife. But the reason this is a tech story, is who she is, and which chips she was close to.

First, why Nvidia matters. Its chips run the data centres behind ChatGPT and Claude. If you've used AI this year, you've probably used Nvidia.

On Thursday, Nvidia filed a notice saying Huang had resigned as CEO, and from the board, effective immediately. His finance chief, Colette Kress, is running the company for now.

Here's what led to it. Huang wrote to staff, quote, "I was unfaithful to my wife. I am deeply sorry." The New York Times reported that US intelligence suspects the woman, a businesswoman named Alice Huang, no relation, of feeding information to China.

And this is the part that matters. She was a senior executive at Megaspeed, a company in Singapore that bought Nvidia's advanced AI chips. Megaspeed is under US criminal investigation over claims it smuggled American chips into China. It denies that.

So the question is no longer about one man's marriage. It's whether the world's most wanted AI chips, and the man who sells them, were a target.

Now the catch. None of that is proven. China calls it a smear. Some US intelligence officials say the evidence doesn't prove she was a spy. And Singapore questioned her, and found insufficient evidence of a crime.

But Nvidia's shares fell about 11 percent this week, roughly 500 billion dollars, and a US senator wants Huang to testify in December.

So here's the thing to remember. The most powerful company in AI just lost its founder, not over chips that failed, but over who got close to the chips.
