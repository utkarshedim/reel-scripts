# 2026-11-13 script: Jensen Huang resigns as Nvidia CEO

## 1. Research

Primary source read: Nvidia's 8-K filing, "NVIDIA Announces CEO Transition", 12 Nov 2026 (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001045810/000104581026000082/nvidiaannouncesceotransiti.htm). It confirms the resignation, the interim CEO and the new chair, and gives no reason.

Other sources read: the CNBC live blog for 12 Nov (stock, Trump, Congress, Huang's apology); NBC News on Megaspeed's statement (the investigation and Alice Huang's role); the PBS NewsHour interview (why chip smuggling matters); the Wikipedia timeline of the scandal, used only to see which claims are confirmed and which are alleged; and NBC Miami / CNBC on the 1993 Denny's founding. AP's report couldn't be fetched, and CNN's live page was blocked (HTTP 451).

## 2. The zest and the angle

**Zest:** The man who built the company behind nearly every AI chip quit overnight, and the trail leads to a Singapore customer accused of sneaking his chips into China.

**Angle:** Most coverage leads with the affair. For this channel, the wait-what is that the scandal loops back to the chips themselves. The US has rules on which Nvidia chips can go to China, and Megaspeed, one of Nvidia's biggest customers in Southeast Asia, is under US criminal investigation for allegedly smuggling them there. The question kept open through the script is "so was she a spy?", and the honest answer is that nobody has proven it. That honesty is the catch.

## 3. Hooks

**Best:** Jensen Huang, the leather jacket guy who built Nvidia, the company whose chips power most of today's AI, just quit, effective immediately. And the trail doesn't stop at an affair. It leads to a company in Singapore accused of smuggling his own chips into China.

**Alternate 1:** Nvidia just lost the founder who ran it since a meeting at a Denny's in 1993. No reason in the filing. But by the end of this, you'll know why the US government cares way more about this than gossip.

**Alternate 2:** Nvidia's founder is out, and the US President wants Apple's Tim Cook to replace him. That's not even the strangest part. The strangest part is where Nvidia's chips may have been going.

## 4. Full script (about 105 to 115 seconds)

Jensen Huang, the leather jacket guy who built Nvidia, the company whose chips power most of today's AI, just quit, effective immediately. And the trail doesn't stop at an affair. It leads to a company in Singapore accused of smuggling his own chips into China.

On November 12th, Nvidia told US regulators that Huang has resigned as CEO and left the board. The filing gives no reason. The CFO, Colette Kress, is now interim CEO. This is a man who started Nvidia with two friends at a Denny's in 1993.

So what happened? Four days earlier, Huang apologised for being, in his words, "unfaithful to my wife." Reports linked him to a woman named Alice Huang, who held a senior job at a company called Megaspeed.

And this is where it becomes a tech story. Megaspeed is based in Singapore, and it's one of Nvidia's biggest customers in Southeast Asia. It's under US criminal investigation for allegedly smuggling American chips into China.

Why does that matter? Because the US limits which AI chips can be sold to China. The most powerful ones decide who builds the most powerful AI. If they leak out, the rules mean nothing.

Now the question everyone's asking. Was she a spy? Some reports, quoting unnamed officials, say US intelligence thinks so. But no proof has been made public, and other officials reportedly disagree. China calls it a smear. Megaspeed hasn't been charged, and says it will cooperate.

The fallout is real though. Nvidia's stock opened down more than 13 percent. Congress wants Huang to testify. And Donald Trump posted that Apple's Tim Cook should take his job.

So here's the one thing to remember. The fight over AI isn't only about who builds the smartest model. It's about where the chips go.

He'd admitted to an affair with a woman who held a senior job at Megaspeed, a Singapore company that's one of Nvidia's biggest customers in Southeast Asia, and that's under US criminal investigation for allegedly smuggling American chips into China.

Was she a spy? That hasn't been proven. But Congress wants answers, because the fight over AI isn't only about the smartest model. It's about where the chips go.
